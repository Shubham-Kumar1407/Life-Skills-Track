# Learning Process

## 1. How to Learn Faster with the Feynman Technique

### Question 1

**What is the Feynman Technique? Explain in 1 line.**

The Feynman Technique is a learning method where you explain a concept in simple language, as if you were teaching a beginner, so you can spot the gaps in your understanding and fix them by going back to the material.


## 2. Learning How to Learn - TED Talk by Barbara Oakley


### Question 2

**In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?**

The most interesting part was Barbara Oakley's own story. She struggled with maths and science as a student, yet she later became a professor of engineering. This shows that learning ability is not fixed. With the right methods and enough practice, anyone can get better at difficult subjects.

Another idea I liked was that taking breaks is part of learning. While we rest, the brain keeps working in the background and connects ideas, which is why solutions often come when we are not actively thinking about the problem.

### Question 3

**What are active and diffused modes of thinking?**

- **Active mode:** The focused mode of thinking. We use it when we concentrate on a specific task, such as reading a concept or solving a problem.
- **Diffused mode:** The relaxed mode of thinking. It happens when we are not focusing on anything in particular, for example while walking, resting or sleeping. In this mode the brain makes broader connections, and new ideas often appear.

Effective learning needs both modes. When we get stuck in the active mode, taking a break lets the diffused mode help.

## Learning Principles in the Bootcamp

### Question 5

**What are some of the actions you can take going forward to improve your learning process?**

1. **Work with full focus:** Keep my phone on silent during work, turn off unnecessary notifications, and block social media during work hours.
2. **Use the Feynman Technique:** Explain every new concept in my own words, in writing or out loud, and revisit the parts I cannot explain simply.
3. **Take regular breaks:** When I am stuck on a problem, step away for a short walk or rest so my brain can use the diffused mode.
4. **Break topics into small parts:** Split a new topic into smaller pieces and practice a little every day.
5. **Understand where a concept is used:** For each concept, first learn where it is applied, then implement it myself in code.
6. **Set deadlines and track time:** Give each topic a time limit and track how I spend my time to stay productive.
7. **Communicate proactively:** If I am stuck, tell my mentor or team early, and explain the problem in clear and precise words.
