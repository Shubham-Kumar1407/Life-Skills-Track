# Tiny Habits

### Question 1. In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

**Answer:** The most interesting idea for me was to make a habit very small. BJ Fogg says to do just two push-ups after using the bathroom. When a habit is this small, it is easy to do every day, and slowly it becomes a big habit.

### Question 2. How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P?

**Answer:** B = MAP means a Behaviour happens when Motivation, Ability and a Prompt come together.

- **M - Motivation:** How much I want to do it.
- **A - Ability:** How easy it is to do.
- **P - Prompt:** A reminder to do it now.

**How to use it:** Make the habit very small so it is easy to do, and do it right after something I already do daily, like "After I brush my teeth, I will do two push-ups."

### Question 3. Why is it important to "Shine" or celebrate after each successful completion of a habit?

**Answer:** Celebrating gives a good feeling right after the habit. The brain remembers this feeling and wants to repeat the habit again. That is why whatever we celebrate becomes a habit. The celebration can be very small, like saying "Well done!" to myself.

### Question 4. In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

**Answer:** The most interesting idea was that small daily improvements add up. If I get 1% better every day, I become about 37 times better in one year. So I should focus on being consistent every day, and not on big changes.

### Question 5. What is the book's perspective about Identity?

**Answer:** The book says real change starts with identity, not goals. Instead of saying "I want to read a book", I should say "I am a reader". Every small action I do is a vote for the type of person I want to become.

### Question 6. Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do.

**Answer:**

- **Make it obvious and attractive:** Set a clear time and place for the habit, and connect it with something I already do or enjoy.
- **Make it easy and satisfying:** Start with a very small version that takes two minutes, and reward myself after doing it.

### Question 7. Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do.

**Answer:**

- **Make it invisible and unattractive:** Remove the things that remind me of the habit, and think about how it harms me.
- **Make it difficult and unsatisfying:** Add extra steps to do the habit, and add a small penalty or ask a friend to check on me.

### Question 8. Pick one habit that you would like to do more of. What are the steps that you can take to make the cue obvious, or the habit more attractive, easy and/or the response satisfying?

**Answer: Practising coding every day.**

I will sit at my desk right after breakfast and start with just a few lines of code. After finishing, I will mark the day on a habit tracker to feel good about my progress.

### Question 9. Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of. What are the steps that you can take to make the cue invisible, the process unattractive or hard, or the response unsatisfying?

**Answer: Scrolling social media too much.**

I will turn off notifications, remove the apps from my home screen, and keep my phone away while I study. I will also set an app timer so it is harder to keep scrolling.
