# Grit and Growth Mindset

### Question 1. Summarize the video in a few (1 or 2) lines. Use your own words.

The video explains that long-term success depends less on talent or IQ and more on grit, which is the passion and perseverance to keep working towards a goal for a long time. Grit can be built by believing that our ability to learn is not fixed and can grow with effort, which is the idea of a growth mindset.

### Question 2. Summarize the video in a few (1 or 2) lines in your own words.

The video explains two types of mindset. People with a fixed mindset believe that skills and intelligence are fixed, so they avoid challenges and give up easily. People with a growth mindset believe that skills can be developed through effort, learning and feedback, so they take on challenges, learn from mistakes and keep improving.

### Question 3. What is the Internal Locus of Control? What is the key point in the video?

- **Internal Locus of Control:** It is the belief that we are in control of our own life and that the results we get come mainly from our own actions and effort, not from luck or outside factors. People with an internal locus of control take responsibility for their outcomes.
- **Key point of the video:** To stay motivated all the time, we should adopt an internal locus of control. When we believe that the outcomes depend on our actions, we feel in control and stay motivated. When we blame external factors, we feel helpless and lose motivation. So we should focus on what we can control and take responsibility for it.

### Question 4. What are the key points mentioned by the speaker to build a growth mindset?

1. Believe in your ability to figure things out.
2. Question your assumptions.
3. Develop your own life curriculum.
4. Honor the struggle.

### Question 5. What are your ideas to take action and build a growth mindset?

1. **Take 100% responsibility for my learning.** I will not blame the topic, the teacher or the situation. If I do not understand something, I will find a way to learn it.
2. **Stay with a problem until I solve it.** I will not quit when it gets hard. I will break the problem into smaller parts, try different approaches and ask for help when needed.
3. **Understand every line of code I write.** I will not copy code that I do not understand. I will make sure I can explain it in my own words.
4. **See struggle as part of learning.** When something feels difficult, I will remind myself that effort is what leads to better understanding, and I will not treat it as a sign that I am not good enough.
5. **Learn from mistakes and feedback.** I will treat errors and bugs as information, and I will ask for feedback and act on it.
6. **Question my own assumptions.** Whenever I think "I can't do this" or "I am bad at this", I will add the word "yet" and look for what I can do to improve.
7. **Focus on what I can control.** I will put my energy into my effort, my time and my attitude instead of things outside my control.
8. **Build consistency.** I will practice a little every day, track my progress, and celebrate small wins to stay motivated over the long term.
